## 2 · The Five-Minute Clock

A clockmaker moved into a sleepy little town, and the first thing he did was fix the old clock tower. For free. The whole town cheered. For the first time in thirty years, the clock was ticking again. There was just one tiny problem. It ran five minutes fast. Every single day. People complained. The mayor wrote him a letter. The clockmaker just smiled and said, "Old towers have a mind of their own." So people got used to it. Every morning, three hundred commuters saw the tower, panicked, and ran to the train station. And every morning, they arrived five minutes early. Five minutes with nothing to do. Cold hands. Empty stomachs. Right next to the platform, a tiny café had just opened. Hot coffee. Warm pastries. Ready in exactly five minutes. Three hundred coffees a day. Fifteen hundred a week. You see, the clockmaker never charged a cent to fix the tower. He didn't have to. The café was his. And to this day, nobody in town has ever missed a train. Or been on time.

## 3 · The Pigeon Man

The town had a pigeon problem. Pigeons on the roofs. Pigeons on the statues. Pigeons on the mayor's hat. So the mayor made an offer: one silver coin for every pigeon removed from town. The next morning, a quiet man in a gray coat showed up with a cage. Ten pigeons. Ten coins. The next day, fifty. Then a hundred. He caught them faster than anyone thought possible. The town called him the Pigeon Man. The mayor called him a hero. Months went by. Five hundred pigeons. A thousand. Two thousand. The town paid and paid. But somehow, there were always more pigeons on the roofs. Nobody could explain it. Until a little girl followed him home one evening. Behind his house stood a giant coop. And every night, he opened the door. You see, they weren't wild pigeons. They were homing pigeons. Trained to fly back to town. And trained to fly back to him. He sold the same two hundred birds to the mayor every single week. And the town paid for every one of them.

## 4 · The Snow Shoveler

Every winter, the town drowned in snow. And every winter, one kid shoveled every driveway on Maple Street. For free. No money. No tips. Just a shovel and a smile. People loved him. The newspaper called him the Snow Angel. Grandmas baked him cookies. One driveway. Ten. Fifty. By January, he'd cleared every driveway in town. But there was something strange. The roads were getting worse. Every morning, there were giant walls of snow in the middle of the street. Cars got stuck. Buses got stuck. The mail stopped coming. So the town did the only thing it could. It hired a snowplow. The only snowplow company in the county. Twenty dollars an hour. All winter long. You see, the kid never charged for driveways. Because every shovel of snow went straight into the road. And the snowplow company? It belonged to his dad. The kid got cookies. His dad got a new truck. And the town got the cleanest driveways it had ever seen.

## 5 · The Free Bread

One morning, every house in town found a fresh loaf of bread on the doorstep. Still warm. No note. No name. The next morning, again. And the next. Free bread. Every single day. Nobody knew who was baking it. People stopped going to the bakeries. Why pay for bread when it shows up for free? The first bakery closed after a month. Then the second. Then the third. By spring, there was only one bakery left in town. A small shop on Church Street, owned by a friendly man named Mr. Crumb. And then, one Monday morning... the doorsteps were empty. No bread. Not that day. Not ever again. So the whole town lined up at the only bakery left. Mr. Crumb smiled and raised his prices. Double. Then triple. You see, the free bread was never free. Mr. Crumb baked every loaf himself. He lost money for six months. And now he owns every slice of bread in town. People still talk about the mystery baker. They just can't afford to.

## 6 · The Rooster Gift

A traveling salesman arrived in town with a strange gift. A free rooster for every family. "Fresh eggs, morning cheer, and good luck all year," he said. Who says no to a free rooster? By sunset, every backyard in town had one. Two hundred roosters. And at four in the morning, all two hundred of them woke up. At once. Every day. Nobody could sleep. Babies cried. Dogs howled. The mayor fell asleep during his own speech. People tried everything. Pillows. Blankets. Cotton in their ears. Nothing worked. And you can't just get rid of a gift. That would be rude. Then, a little shop opened next to the town hall. Earplugs. Two silver coins a pair. They sold out in a single morning. Then again. And again. You see, roosters don't lay eggs. Only hens do. The salesman knew that. And the man selling earplugs? He had the same hat, the same mustache, and the same smile. Two hundred roosters. Free. Two thousand earplugs. Not free.
